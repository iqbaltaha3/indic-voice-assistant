# Land Purchase Due-Diligence Checklist

This is a general checklist. Exact document names vary by state (see the
state-specific files), but the underlying checks apply nationwide.

## 1. Confirm the seller's title
- Obtain and read the seller's original title document (sale deed, gift
  deed, partition deed, or succession/legal heir certificate as
  applicable) going back at least 12-30 years where possible ("chain of
  title").
- Confirm the seller's name matches exactly across the title document, the
  revenue record (khata/patta/khasra), and their government ID.

## 2. Check the Encumbrance Certificate (EC)
- Obtain an EC from the Sub-Registrar's office covering at least the last
  13-30 years.
- A "nil encumbrance" EC for that period is a strong positive signal; any
  listed mortgage, lien, or unresolved transaction needs to be explained
  and cleared before proceeding.

## 3. Verify the revenue record matches
- Cross-check the survey/khasra/CTS number, area, and current
  owner/cultivator name on the relevant state land record (RTC, 7/12,
  Khatauni, Patta, Pahani, etc.) against the title document.
- Confirm mutation has already been completed in the seller's name from
  their own purchase/inheritance -- an un-mutated seller record is a red
  flag.

## 4. Check for litigation or government acquisition
- Ask for a "no-litigation" or "non-encumbrance" declaration and, where
  possible, independently check local court records or consult a local
  lawyer for pending land disputes.
- Confirm the land is not marked for government acquisition, is not part
  of a protected/reserved category (forest land, government "poramboke"/
  waste land, etc.), and is not under any land ceiling litigation.

## 5. Confirm physical possession and boundaries
- Physically visit the site, verify boundaries against the survey
  document, and confirm the seller (or their tenant) is in actual
  possession -- a mismatch between paper records and physical possession
  is a serious warning sign.

## 6. Complete registration and mutation after purchase
- Register the sale deed at the Sub-Registrar's office (registration is
  what legally transfers ownership, not just payment or an unregistered
  agreement).
- Apply for mutation/patta transfer/khata transfer immediately afterward
  so the revenue record reflects the new owner -- do not delay this step.

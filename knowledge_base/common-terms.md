# Common Land Record Terms (India-wide)

These terms appear across most Indian states, though the exact document name
and issuing authority varies by state (see state-specific files for those).

## Survey Number / Khasra Number
A unique number assigned to a specific plot of land by the revenue
department during a land survey. Every due-diligence check on a property
should start by confirming the survey number matches across all documents
(sale deed, tax receipts, mutation record) -- mismatched survey numbers are
one of the most common signs of a disputed or fraudulently described plot.

## Khata
An account or record maintained by the local municipal or revenue authority
that identifies who is registered as the owner/occupant of a property for
taxation purposes. A khata is not the same as a title deed -- it proves the
property is registered for tax purposes under a name, not that the person is
the legal owner. Two khata types commonly exist: "A khata" (fully compliant,
approved layout) and "B khata" (irregular or unapproved layout) in some
states like Karnataka -- properties with only a B khata often carry legal and
resale risk.

## Mutation (Dakhil Kharij / Intkal)
The process of updating land revenue records to reflect a change in
ownership -- after a sale, inheritance, or gift. Mutation does NOT itself
transfer legal title (that happens via a registered sale deed); it only
updates who pays land revenue/tax and who the record shows as the current
holder. A buyer should always confirm mutation has been completed in their
name after a purchase, since an un-mutated record can cause serious problems
years later.

## Encumbrance Certificate (EC)
A certificate issued by the Sub-Registrar's office listing all registered
transactions on a property over a specified period -- sales, mortgages,
leases, gifts. A clean EC (showing "nil encumbrance") for the period in
question is one of the most important checks before buying land, since it
reveals whether the property is mortgaged, under litigation-related
attachment, or has other registered claims against it.

## Title Deed vs Sale Deed
A "title deed" is a general term for any document that establishes ownership
(this could be a sale deed, gift deed, partition deed, or a will/succession
certificate). A "sale deed" specifically is the registered document created
when a property changes hands through a purchase -- it is the single most
important document proving current legal ownership from a sale.

## Registered vs Unregistered Documents
Under the Indian Registration Act, transactions of immovable property above
a certain value must be registered with the Sub-Registrar to be legally
valid and enforceable. An unregistered sale agreement (even if notarized)
generally does not transfer ownership and offers very weak legal protection
-- this is a common trap in informal land deals.

## Power of Attorney (POA) Sales
Buying land where the seller is represented only through a Power of
Attorney (rather than the actual owner signing) carries elevated risk of
fraud, especially "General Power of Attorney" (GPA) sales, which courts have
repeatedly held do not by themselves transfer ownership. Always verify the
POA is registered, specific (not general), and still valid, and ideally
have the actual owner also confirm the transaction.

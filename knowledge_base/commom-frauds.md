# Common Land Fraud Patterns in India

## Selling the same land to multiple buyers
A fraudulent seller sells the same plot to more than one buyer using
unregistered agreements or forged documents, before any single buyer
completes registration and mutation -- which is why prompt registration and
mutation after purchase (not delaying it) meaningfully reduces this risk.

## General Power of Attorney (GPA) sales
Selling land through a General Power of Attorney, sometimes from an agent
who no longer represents the actual owner, or using a GPA that has expired
or been revoked. Indian courts have repeatedly held that GPA-based
"sales" without a proper registered sale deed do not validly transfer
ownership. Always verify a POA is registered, specific, and current, and
where possible confirm directly with the original owner.

## Forged or fabricated documents
Fabricated sale deeds, forged signatures, or altered survey records,
especially for land that has been inherited across many family members
without formal partition, or land where the true owner lives far away and
is unlikely to notice.

## Selling government, forest, or "poramboke" (common/waste) land
Encroached government land, forest land, or common village land is
sometimes sold to unsuspecting buyers using forged private ownership
documents -- always independently verify the land's classification with the
revenue department rather than relying solely on the seller's paperwork.

## Selling disputed or litigation land without disclosure
Land under an ongoing inheritance dispute, partition suit, or other
litigation is sold without disclosing the dispute -- an encumbrance
certificate alone will not always reveal unregistered or informal family
disputes, so asking neighbors/local inquiry and getting a lawyer's opinion
matters.

## Benami transactions
Property purchased in the name of one person (the "benami" holder) while
actually being paid for and controlled by someone else, often to conceal
the true buyer's identity -- this is illegal under the Benami Transactions
(Prohibition) Act and carries serious legal risk for all parties involved.
